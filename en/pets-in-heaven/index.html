On this page

“After they died, the only thing I really wanted to know was this: are they okay? Will we ever see each other again?”

The Bible doesn't answer every question in one sentence — but it gives enough light to see hope even in the darkest night.

Before you begin

This is not a place that asks you to "move on" quickly. These four readings are for you — still missing them.

Losing an animal companion is a particular kind of grief. People say things like "it was just a dog" or "just get another one" — but you know that isn't true. They knew the sound of your footsteps coming home. In your hardest year, they stayed pressed against your feet. What left with them was the first pair of eyes that greeted you every morning.

The Bible actually says a great deal about animals. God created them, feeds them, makes a covenant with them, and places them inside the world He promises to renew. These verses don't prove that "every pet is in heaven" — the Bible doesn't promise that — but they reveal something tender: in God's plan, animals were never disposable extras.

May what you find here be more than verses — may it be the God who remembers even a sparrow, remembering yours.

Four readings for you

Will I See My Pet Again?

The hope of the Bible for animals and heaven — creation itself waits for redemption, and the new creation still has animals in it.

Romans 8:19-22 · Isaiah 11:6-9 · Revelation 21:4-5

Not One Sparrow Is Forgotten

They were so small — will the world remember them? Jesus said not one sparrow falls without your Father knowing.

Matthew 10:29-31 · Psalm 147:9 · Psalm 36:6

Beyond the Rainbow Bridge

“Rainbow Bridge” is a beautiful image — and the Bible really does have a rainbow: God's everlasting covenant with every living creature.

Genesis 9:9-13 · Isaiah 65:25 · Revelation 5:13

Permission to Grieve

God keeps your tears in a bottle. Grief is not a lack of faith — it is love still going on.

Psalm 56:8 · Psalm 34:18 · Matthew 5:4

How to read these

🕯️

Slowly — not all at once

One paragraph is enough. If a verse makes you cry, stop there. That isn't weakness; that is love moving.

📝

Put their name into the verse

When you read “He gives to the beast its food,” try their name in that sentence. God cared for them like that, too.

🕊️

Say one thing to them afterwards

It doesn't need to be beautiful. “I miss you” is enough. If you want to say more, the guided journal in PetLoss will sit with you.
